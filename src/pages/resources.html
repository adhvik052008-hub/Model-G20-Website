The one document you must write

Position papers

Due 23:59 IST, 5 November 2026. Required for every committee except the International Press Corps, which submits a portfolio instead.

A position paper is not an essay about a country. It is a statement of what your delegation will and will not accept, written so that another delegation can find the deal in it.

I. The problem as your delegation sees it. Two paragraphs. Not a history of the agenda — your national reading of why the current position is unacceptable.

II. Your delegation's position. The core of the paper. What you propose, what you will fund, what you require in return, and the specific language you want in the outcome document.

III. Where you can move. The section most delegates omit and every chair reads first. Name the concessions you can make and the ones you cannot. This is what makes you findable in caucus.

One side of A4, single-spaced, 11pt. Between 600 and 800 words. Header carries the committee, the delegation and the delegate's name. Footnoted citations in any consistent style; a works-cited list does not count toward the word limit.

Submit as PDF, named COMMITTEE_COUNTRY_SURNAME.pdf. Papers submitted in any other format are returned unread.

Every paper is screened. Uncited copying from any source — including another delegate's paper, a previous conference, or an AI assistant — disqualifies the paper from award consideration and is reported to the delegate's faculty advisor.

Using a research tool to find sources is fine. Submitting text you did not write and cannot defend on the floor is not, and it becomes obvious in the first moderated caucus.

You may still attend and participate fully. You are not eligible for an award in that committee. There is no appeal and no extension — the deadline exists so that chairs can read every paper before the first session, and a late paper cannot be read in time.

Recognition

How awards are judged

Five awards per committee. The bench judges on four criteria, weighted equally, across all three days — not on the closing speech.

01

Substance

Command of the agenda and of your own country's real position, including its inconvenient parts.

02

Diplomacy

Whether other delegations want to negotiate with you by day three, and whether you kept the ones you needed.

03

Caucus

What you actually did in unmoderated time. Blocs built, language brokered, deadlocks broken.

04

The document

Your fingerprints on the outcome. Clauses authored, amendments carried, and whether it reached consensus.

The conference

Not a school event.
A summit.

The G20 is the only forum where the world's largest economies negotiate without a veto, without a charter, and without the option of walking away. Model G20 2026 reproduces that pressure exactly: ten committees running in parallel, live crisis, an accredited press corps, and a Leaders' Declaration that only carries if the room agrees.

Delegates arrive holding a national brief and leave having defended it against nineteen others. Some will find the consensus. Most will find out what it costs.

What the summit is built on

Five commitments

Every committee, every document and every hour of floor time is designed against these five. They are the standard the Secretariat is held to.

01

Diplomacy

Procedure taught properly, then enforced. Delegates learn the difference between a speech and a position, and why only one of them moves a room.

02

Global Leadership

Every seat carries a real country's constraints — its debt, its coalitions, its electorate. Leadership here means carrying those and still building a majority.

03

Innovation

A dedicated Digital Economy Working Group negotiating frontier AI governance — compute thresholds, cross-border data, and the sovereignty questions underneath both.

04

Sustainability

Two committees on the transition, and a hard rule: no climate outcome is adopted without a costed finance annex attached to it.

05

Youth Leadership

The Y20 communiqué is formally transmitted to the Leaders' Summit and may be cited on the floor — the only engagement group with that standing.

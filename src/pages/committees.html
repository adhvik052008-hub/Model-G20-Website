Summit/Committees

Ten committees. One declaration.

Two tracks, an engagement group, a press corps and a live crisis. Every committee feeds language upward; the Leaders' Summit inherits what the tracks could and could not settle.

Check seat availability
Difficulty

Every committee carries a grading from Foundational to Advanced. Foundational committees assume no prior experience and teach procedure from the first session. Advanced committees assume you already know it.

Seats

Seat counts reflect the real membership of each forum. The Leaders' Summit and Finance Track seat G20 members only; the working groups seat invited states alongside them; the Development Working Group and Y20 seat the full floor including organisations.

Outputs

Each committee produces a named document that is transmitted to the Leaders' Summit on the final day. Awards are judged in part on your contribution to that document, not only on how often you spoke.

How the tracks connect

Language moves upward

Nothing at this summit is negotiated in isolation. The working groups draft ministerial language; the Finance Track negotiates in parallel on its own communiqué; both are transmitted to the Leaders' Summit, which must reconcile them into a single declaration.

Level 1Sessions I–IV

Working groups draft

ECSWG, DEWG, TIWG, DWG and ETWG each produce ministerial language on their own agenda, with a costed annex where finance is engaged.

Level 2Session V

Finance Track reconciles

FMCBG reviews every finance annex arriving from the Sherpa Track and rules on what the numbers will actually bear.

Level 3Session VI

Leaders adopt or return

The Leaders' Summit adopts transmitted language into the Declaration, or returns it to committee with an instruction. It may not rewrite it unilaterally.

ParallelThroughout

Y20, Press and Crisis run alongside

The Y20 communiqué is transmitted and may be cited on the floor. The Press Corps files daily. Crisis directives can and do change what the other committees are negotiating about.

The premise

Why the G20

Almost every conference in the circuit simulates the United Nations. The United Nations has a charter, a permanent secretariat, a Security Council and a veto. The G20 has none of these.

It has no founding treaty and no headquarters. Its presidency rotates annually. It cannot compel a member to do anything, and any member may simply decline. What it does have is the room where nineteen countries, the European Union and the African Union — together roughly eighty-five per cent of world GDP and two-thirds of the world's population — sit down without a hierarchy between them.

That makes it the hardest kind of negotiation to simulate well, and the most instructive. Nothing is procedurally enforced. Every outcome is voluntary. A delegation gets what it wants only by convincing the room that agreeing is worth more than winning.

There is no gavel that can make twenty sovereign governments agree. There is only the argument.

How it differs

Four departures from the standard format

01

Consensus, not majority

No committee at this summit adopts a document by simple majority. Outcome documents carry by consensus or they do not carry. A single sustained objection is enough to hold the floor — and delegates learn very quickly what it costs to use that power, and what it costs not to.

02

Two tracks, one declaration

The real G20 runs a Sherpa Track and a Finance Track that must reconcile before leaders meet. So does this one. Working groups feed language upward, the Finance Track negotiates in parallel, and the Leaders' Summit inherits whatever the tracks could and could not settle.

03

A press corps with teeth

The International Press Corps is not decorative. It sits in every committee, publishes a daily edition before the morning session, and runs the leaders' press conference live. Delegates are quoted, and quotes are held against them on the floor.

04

Live crisis, no warning

The Emergency Sherpa Session convenes under the Chair's emergency powers on the morning of day two. No background guide is issued in advance. The situation escalates on a timer, and directives are drafted against it in real time.

The 2026 mandate

Common Ground

Rebuilding trust in a fragmented world.

Three consecutive presidencies have produced declarations that agreed on the diagnosis and split on the remedy. Supply chains are being rebuilt along political lines. Debt distress is deepening in the countries with the least fiscal room. AI capability is concentrating faster than any governance framework can follow. And the institutions built to arbitrate all of it — the WTO's appellate body, the Bretton Woods quota system, the Security Council — are each, in their own way, stuck.

The mandate for this edition is deliberately narrow and deliberately hard: find what is still negotiable. Not what should be. What is.

The convenor

The School of Raya

The School of Raya convenes Model G20 as its flagship academic conference. The programme is student-led and faculty-supervised: the Secretariat is appointed from the student body, chairs are trained and certified before summit, and every background guide is reviewed by the Office of Committee Affairs before release.

The conference is open to delegates from any school, college or university, in India or abroad, whether or not they have sat at a Model conference before.

The venue

FLAME University, Pune

The summit is hosted on FLAME University's campus at Lavale, in the hills north-west of Pune. Ten committee rooms, a plenary auditorium, a dedicated press centre and on-campus accommodation sit within a single walkable perimeter — the whole summit takes place inside one campus, which is exactly what a two-day negotiation needs.

The campus is roughly forty-five minutes from Pune Airport and thirty from Pune Junction. All committee spaces are step-free and lift-served.

The delegate journey

Seven steps between deciding to apply and standing for the closing plenary.

01 Choose a committee Ten committees graded from Foundational to Advanced. Read the agendas before you rank them.
02 Register Three committee preferences, three country preferences, individually or as a delegation.
03 Receive allotment Released on a rolling basis. Most applicants receive one of their first two choices.
04 Prepare Background guide, rules of procedure, and the position paper due 1 October.
05 Accredit Credentials, placard and delegate kit issued on the morning of 8 October.
06 Negotiate Five committee sessions, one crisis, one press conference, two days.
07 Adopt & transmit Outcome documents adopted, transmitted to the Leaders' Summit, awards announced.
